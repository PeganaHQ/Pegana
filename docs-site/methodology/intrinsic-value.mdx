---
title: "Intrinsic value"
description: "What an asset is supposed to be worth, per its own mechanism. Different formula per asset class."
---

Intrinsic value is the answer to "what should 1 unit of this asset return if I redeem
it through the canonical path?" — not the secondary market, not the DEX, the redemption
mechanism itself.

It is **always quoted in USD** at the engine boundary (so spreads are comparable), but
each asset class has a different source of truth.

## SOL-pegged LSTs

Source: **Sanctum's** `sol_value` per LST mint — or, for LSTs outside the Sanctum
registry, a direct **on-chain SPL stake-pool read** (`total_lamports / pool_token_supply`).

```text
intrinsic_sol(LST) = sanctum.sol_value(LST mint)
intrinsic_usd(LST) = intrinsic_sol × pyth.SOL/USD
```

Polled every 15s. Each LST has its own deterministic `sol_value` — for jitoSOL today,
1 jitoSOL ≈ 1.18 SOL because the underlying stake pool has accrued staking + MEV
rewards since the asset was minted.

We track **43 LSTs** — major Sanctum-registry pools (`jitoSOL`, `mSOL`, `bSOL`, `INF`,
`JupSOL`, `BNSOL`, `PSOL`, `JSOL`, `hSOL`, …) plus the Hylo LSTs (`hyloSOL`, `hyloSOL+`).
LSTs not in Sanctum's registry — e.g. `STKESOL`, `rkuSOL`, `corvusSOL` — use the
on-chain stake-pool read described above instead. The full live list is always
[`GET /v1/assets`](/guides/rest-api).

## Fiat stables

Source: the **issuer's contract** = `$1`, no exception.

USDC, USDT, PYUSD, USDG, USD1, USDu, CASH peg to $1 by design. The intrinsic is therefore
constant. The signal lives entirely on the market side — if USDC trades at $0.998, that's
a `-2 bps` spread; if it trades at $0.87 (the 2023 SVB weekend), that's `-1300 bps`.

`USDS` (a CDP-style stable, `stable_cdp`) and `USDe` (a delta-neutral synthetic stable,
`stable_dn`) are **not** fiat stables — their intrinsic is not a flat issuer-contract $1.
They are covered by the CDP and delta-neutral paths, not this group.

We do not introduce a hardcoded `1.0` in the schema — the intrinsic comes from the
configured peg target (`USD`, `EUR`, etc.) and is read like any other FX rate.

## Yield-bearing NAV stables

Source: the issuer's own NAV, read from wherever the issuer publishes it:

- **Issuer contract on Ethereum**, read with a plain `eth_call` (USDY, sUSDe). USDY
  uses Ondo's `RWADynamicOracle.getPrice()`; sUSDe uses the sUSDe ERC-4626 vault's
  `convertToAssets(1e18)` (USDe per sUSDe, with USDe treated as $1). The call is pinned
  to a block no more than 180s old. At least two of three independent public RPCs
  must agree within 10 bps, or nothing is published; one answering RPC is never enough.
- **On-chain Solana decode**: the Jupiter Perpetuals pool (JLP:
  `aum_usd / JLP supply`, read together in one call, refused if the pool's AUM has not
  been refreshed for 5 minutes), the mint's Token-2022 InterestBearing extension
  (sUSD), or the Perena vault's `mint_share_price` (`USD*`, with the vault's
  `last_update_ts` as the liveness signal).
- **Pyth Redemption Rate** feeds, for issuers that still publish one there (for
  example syrupUSDC).

```text
intrinsic_usd(USDY)  = eth_call(ondo.RWADynamicOracle.getPrice()) / 1e18
intrinsic_usd(sUSDe) = eth_call(sUSDe.convertToAssets(1e18)) / 1e18
intrinsic_usd(JLP)   = perps_pool.aum_usd / jlp_mint.supply
intrinsic_usd(sUSD)  = decode_token_2022_rebasing(sUSD_mint)
```

The market leg never comes from the issuer's own mint or redeem route: the Jupiter
quote for `USD*` excludes `Perena Star V2`, Perena's own share swap. `USD*` has no
independent deep market yet, so it stays monitoring-only and reads `UNKNOWN`.

JLP is the exception to read with care. Its market leg is the Pyth JLP/USD spot price,
whose derivation Pyth does not document, and JLP can be minted and redeemed at the pool
NAV through the Jupiter Perpetuals program. That market price is therefore likely
arbitrage-coupled to the NAV rather than independent of it, so a JLP discount is weaker
evidence than for an asset with its own market. The wide 200 bps drift band is the guard. sUSDe has only about $2.4k of
liquidity on Solana, so its market leg is a **global** sUSDe price, not a Solana-venue
price.

The NAV grows over time: USDY's NAV sat around $1.15 as of 2026-10 because the
underlying T-bill strategy has kept earning since launch. Treating these as $1-pegged would systematically
under-value the position.

For issuers that haven't published a Pyth feed yet, we scrape the dashboard NAV and tag
the asset `verified=false` in `assets.toml` until a real feed lands. (Piggybank `pbUSDC`
was an example of this path but was **deactivated 2026-06-01** — only a thin dust DEX pair
remained, so its prices were no longer reliable.)

## CDP-style stables

Source: **on-chain decode** of the protocol's collateral state.

For Hylo's hyUSD:

```text
total_sol = decode(hylo.exchange_pda.total_sol_cache.total_sol)
collateral_usd = total_sol × pyth.SOL/USD
intrinsic_usd(hyUSD) = collateral_usd / supply(hyUSD mint)
```

Polled every 30s. The intrinsic here is effectively the collateral ratio — a hyUSD
that trades at $1.000 with a 130% CR is in a different risk state than a hyUSD that
trades at $1.000 with a 160% CR.

See [Hylo CDP CR signal](/concepts/hylo-cdp-cr-signal) for the alert mapping.

## FX-pegged assets

Source: the **EUR/USD spot rate from two free public venues**, multiplied by the
issuer's redemption claim.

A EUR-pegged stable should redeem at `EUR/USD` per token. Drift from that is the spread.

```text
eur_usd = median(kraken.EURUSD mid(bid, ask), coinbase.exchange_rates(EUR).USD)
```

Both venues must answer and agree within 10 bps. If either one is missing or they
disagree, nothing is published and the asset ages out to stale. We track EURC (Circle
Euro Coin), the live EUR-pegged asset: its intrinsic redeems at `eur_usd` per token.
EURC is monitoring_only and dark on the forex weekend. Spot FX does not trade from
Friday ~22:00 to Sunday ~22:00 UTC, so we do not publish an FX intrinsic between
Fri 20:30 and Sun 22:30 UTC (a buffer that covers both DST regimes), even though crypto
venues keep quoting. That staleness is expected, not an outage.

## Why one source per class

Two reasons:

1. **Determinism.** When you ship a single alert that says "USDC entered DEPEG at
   14:32:11," subscribers need to be able to reproduce that decision from raw inputs.
   A blended price (Pyth + DEX1 + DEX2, weighted somehow) is reproducible only with
   the weighting algorithm published — and even then is harder to argue from.

2. **Failure mode honesty.** If Sanctum's `sol_value` is wrong, every Pegana LST signal
   is wrong, and we want to know loudly. If we blended Sanctum with a DEX-side LST
   redemption proxy, a Sanctum bug would be masked by the proxy. We prefer the loud
   failure.

The cost is dependency on a single upstream. We accept it — and on divergence
(IDL drift on Hylo, a stale Pyth push account, polling staleness on Sanctum) the engine
**skips the recompute and holds the last published state** rather than emitting a
confident-but-wrong number. (`UNKNOWN` is published at cold-start and as honest-dark on a
detectably broken anchor — a >10% premium on a NAV-priced asset, via the NAV-sanity gate
(ADR-0025) — not on routine staleness; containment of unreliable sources is via
`monitoring_only` — ADR-0024.)
