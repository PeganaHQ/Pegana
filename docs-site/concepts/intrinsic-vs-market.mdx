---
title: "Intrinsic vs market"
description: "Every Pegana signal is the gap between two numbers — what the asset's mechanism says it's worth, and what the market actually pays for it."
---

The framing that makes peg monitoring tractable is **two numbers, side by side**:

- **Intrinsic** — what the asset *should* be worth, per its own mechanism
- **Market** — what you'd actually receive swapping it

Every other concept in Pegana — discount, state, threshold, alert — is derived from
the gap between these two.

## Why both, not one

A monitor that reads only **market** misses the case where the market is the broken
side. The [mSOL/Pyth incident](/case-studies/msol-pyth-may-2022) is the canonical
example: Marinade's on-chain redemption (intrinsic) stayed at 1.18 SOL while a thin
Mango pool printed 0.85 SOL for four minutes. A market-only oracle would have said
"mSOL just crashed." The right answer was "the market lied for 4 minutes; intrinsic
held."

A monitor that reads only **intrinsic** misses the [USDC/SVB case](/case-studies/usdc-svb-march-2023):
Circle's redemption claim was always $1, but the secondary market priced doubt at
$0.87 for 36 hours. An intrinsic-only oracle would have said "USDC is fine." The
right answer was "intrinsic still claims $1, but the market won't pay it until the
backstop arrives."

Holding both numbers in the same signal — and publishing both — lets the consumer
decide which side to act on for their use case.

## How each side is sourced

**Intrinsic** comes from the asset's own mechanism. Each class has its own canonical
source: Sanctum for LSTs; the issuer's own NAV for yield-bearing (Ondo's
RWADynamicOracle and the sUSDe ERC-4626 vault read on Ethereum via `eth_call`, on-chain
decode for JLP, sUSD and `USD*`, Pyth Redemption Rate for the rest); on-chain decode
for CDPs; a free two-venue FX median for fiat-pegged (EURC). See [intrinsic value](/methodology/intrinsic-value)
for the full table.

**Market** comes from Jupiter's routed quote, converted to USD via Pyth. We always
swap *to* USDC and reprice USDC → USD via Pyth, so the numeraire is consistent across
every asset and a USDC depeg doesn't contaminate other assets' spreads. See
[market value](/methodology/market-value).

## The two-numbers payload

Every Pegana peg reading carries both numbers explicitly. An illustrative
`GET /v1/assets/jitoSOL/state` response (values are examples, not a live reading):

```json
{
  "asset": "jitoSOL",
  "state": "PEGGED",
  "since": "2026-05-26T14:32:11.000Z",
  "discount": "0.0034",
  "intrinsic_usd": "213.42",
  "market_usd": "212.69",
  "updated_at": "2026-05-26T14:40:02.000Z",
  "stale": false,
  "confidence": "high"
}
```

You receive the spread (`discount`) for fast checks and the underlying numbers for
your own logic. For LSTs the asset card (`GET /v1/assets/{symbol}`) also carries
`sol_per_lst`, the intrinsic SOL-per-token ratio behind `intrinsic_usd`.

## Sign convention

```
discount = 1 − market_usd / intrinsic_usd
```

- `discount > 0` → market trades **below** intrinsic (most common stress case)
- `discount < 0` → market trades **above** intrinsic (less common; often the LST
  case where MEV-driven yield outpaces the published `sol_value`)
- `|discount| < threshold` → `PEGGED`

A common confusion: the API and the dashboard sign the same spread differently. The API
`discount` field keeps the raw sign above, so `"0.0120"` means the market is offering the
asset 1.20% below its book value (the trading-floor sense of "discount"). The dashboard shows
the holder's view of that same number, `−1.20%`: a holder marked to market is down 1.20% versus
fair value. A premium is the reverse: a negative API `discount`, shown as `+` on the dashboard.

## When intrinsic and market converge by construction

For a `PEGGED` fiat stable, intrinsic = $1 and market ≈ $1, so the spread hovers
inside ±10 bps. That's the calm regime — and it's the regime where the two-numbers
framing looks redundant. ("Just publish market — what could intrinsic add?")

The framing pays off precisely when they diverge. Holding both numbers in the same
schema means the divergence is **structural**, not an afterthought. Your downstream
consumer never has to wonder which number to act on — both are there.
